To translate a Google Slides presentation, you copy the deck once per language and replace the text in every text box, table cell, and speaker note with its translation. It's hard because translated text rarely takes the same space as the original (a short English label can run much longer in German, as the W3C's guide to [text size in translation](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size) shows), so text overflows its box or leaves gaps that someone has to fix slide by slide. Each copy also goes out of date as soon as someone edits the source deck.

## Ways to translate a presentation

| Approach | What it does well | Where the translation ends up | What it leaves to you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Translating a copy by hand | A translator sees each slide and can shorten text to fit | A copy you make and name for each language | Every text box, table cell, and speaker note in every language, and finding what changed after each source edit |
| Document translation in Google Translate or DeepL | Translates a whole `.pptx` file in one upload | A downloaded `.pptx` that you convert back to Slides as a separate presentation | Checking every slide after the conversion, including text that no longer fits, and repeating the round trip after each edit |
| A script on the Google Slides API with an AI model (OpenAI API, Claude API) | Takes your glossary and tone rules in the prompt, for teams that already script their decks | Wherever your script writes it | Rewriting text in shapes, table cells, and speaker notes without losing its styling, refitting text that grew or shrank, and rerunning after edits |
| General Translation (Google Drive integration) | Translates text in shapes, table cells, and speaker notes, keeps the source's styling, links, and lists, and runs a formatting pass, billed per slide, that fits the new text to each slide | A separate presentation per language in the source's folder, with images, videos, and charts left as they are | Checking slides where text now overflows or wraps differently, and running **Apply changes** after the source changes |

Use a hand-translated copy when one deck goes into one or two languages and a translator can fit each slide. Use document translation when you only need to read a deck in another language, and a script only when you already automate decks through the Slides API. Use General Translation's Google Drive integration when decks change and go out in several languages, so every copy has to keep its layout and follow the source.

## Translate a presentation with General Translation

General Translation's Google Drive integration translates presentations from the Dashboard and requires a Starter or Enterprise plan.

1. Open **Project > Integrations > Catalog** and click **Connect** on **Google Drive**.
2. Click **Connect account** and sign in to Google.
3. Click **Add files** and pick the presentation.
4. Check its **Source** language and choose languages under **Translate into**.
5. Click **Translate**.

## Update translations after the source changes

After you edit the source, **Apply changes** rebuilds each copy, reuses translations whose source text hasn't changed, and keeps your edits to a copy where the source text still matches. In General Translation, the same Context Group (a Glossary and Custom Prompts) can guide your decks, app, website, and docs, so a product name translates the same way in each. The [Google Drive Quickstart](/docs/integrations/google-drive/quickstart) has the full steps.

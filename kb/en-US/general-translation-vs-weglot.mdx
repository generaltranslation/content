Weglot is a website translation layer: it detects the text on your rendered pages, machine-translates it, and serves each language through a CMS plugin, a JavaScript snippet, or a reverse proxy. General Translation is a localization platform: it translates at the source in your codebase, so each language is part of the build you test and ship. Weglot suits a finished marketing site nobody codes; an app developers change every week needs translation in the release itself.

## What Weglot is

Weglot's [technical overview](https://weglot.com/technical-presentation/) lists three ways in: a JavaScript snippet, a reverse proxy that serves each language on a subdomain or subdirectory, and integrations for WordPress, Shopify, Wix, BigCommerce, Webflow, Squarespace, and Drupal. It [picks DeepL, Google Translate, or Microsoft for each language pair](https://support.weglot.com/article/114-available-languages), and you edit the output in its translation list or visual editor. From the Starter plan up, you can [order professional translation](https://support.weglot.com/article/198-how-to-order-professional-translations).

Weglot's [pricing](https://weglot.com/pricing) is a subscription tiered by translated words and target languages. Words are [counted per target language](https://support.weglot.com/article/59-total-number-translated-words), so 8,000 source words in three languages come to about 24,000 translated words. The limit doesn't renew monthly, and translation stops at it unless auto-upgrade is on.

## Weglot vs. General Translation at a glance

| | Weglot | General Translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category | Website translation layer | Localization platform: i18n libraries, CLI, Dashboard, and [Locadex](/docs/platform/locadex/quickstart) |
| How it connects | Proxy (DNS), JavaScript snippet, or CMS integration; React package in preview | Open-source SDKs in your code, the [`gt` CLI](/docs/cli/quickstart), and Locadex pull requests |
| What gets translated | Text detected on the rendered page | Content marked with [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t) or `gt()`, plus files such as JSON, Markdown, and HTML |
| Context | The surrounding page text, plus glossary rules that always or never translate a term | JSX structure, [`context`](/docs/react/reference/components/t#context) notes, and Context Groups shared across projects |
| Where translations live | Weglot's database; export needs the Advanced plan | Files in your build or General Translation's CDN |
| Language URLs and hreflang | Subdomain or subdirectory with hreflang; the snippet and React package keep one URL per page | [`createNextMiddleware`](/docs/react/nextjs/reference/functions/create-next-middleware) serves `/de/about` and localized slugs from your app |
| Review | Translation list and visual editor | Dashboard review with annotations, version history, and roles |
| Continuous translation | New page content detected and translated automatically after it's live; stored translations reused for identical text | Translates only changed content in CI; Locadex opens pull requests |
| Professional translation | Order in Weglot | Your team edits in the Dashboard |
| Pricing | Subscription tiers by translated words and languages | [Usage-based, with no seat charges](https://generaltranslation.com/pricing) |

## What Weglot doesn't cover

### Translation in your release

Translations live in Weglot's database and are edited in its dashboard, outside your repository, and [exporting them](https://support.weglot.com/article/432-what-can-we-export-import) takes the Advanced plan or higher. New copy is translated after it reaches the rendered page, not in the build you test.

With General Translation, you wrap copy in [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t), or let the compiler [wrap JSX automatically](/docs/cli/guides/using-auto-jsx), then run [`npx gt translate`](/docs/cli/reference/commands/translate) before `next build`. It translates only content whose source changed, and Locadex can open the translation pull request for you.

### Indexable languages from your own app

Weglot says its JavaScript integration "does not offer SEO benefits," because translations are inserted in the browser. Indexable languages need its WordPress [plugin](https://support.weglot.com/article/99-wordpress-how-weglot-manage-seo), or its subdomain or subdirectory integration, which serve pages through Weglot's proxy. [`@weglot/translate-react`](https://developers.weglot.com/frameworks-and-ai/react) is in preview, and its docs say all languages share the same URLs, so search engines index the source language only.

General Translation's `gt-next` middleware serves each language at its own URL, such as `/de/ueber-uns`, and [`getGT`](/docs/react/nextjs/reference/functions/get-gt) translates titles and descriptions inside `generateMetadata`. No proxy sits in front of your site. (See [language redirects and SEO](/kb/locale-routing-seo).)

### Context and consistency

Weglot translates detected page text with [glossary rules](https://support.weglot.com/article/130-translations-exceptions) that make a term always or never translate. General Translation's [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t) keeps a sentence together with its markup and variables, [`<Var>`](/docs/react/reference/components/var) keeps dynamic values out of translation, and [`<Plural>`](/docs/react/reference/components/plural) follows each locale's rules. A [Context Group](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/defining-context-for-translations) applies the same Glossary and Custom Prompts to your app, docs, and marketing site, and a consistency check compares new translations with existing ones.

### Review and runtime dependency

General Translation's [Dashboard](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/reviewing-translations) shows each translation next to its source, with annotations, version history, and roles; Enterprise plans add SSO and audit logs. Its libraries are MIT-licensed and work without an account, and an app that ships translations with its build makes no network calls to General Translation at run time.

Cursor localizes its web product and docs into 14+ locales with General Translation. (See [production deployments](https://generaltranslation.com/enterprise).)

## When Weglot makes sense

- **A site builder or CMS nobody codes.** The site runs on WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, or Wix, and no developer maintains it. General Translation doesn't offer plugins for those builders.
- **Languages live before a release.** A marketing team needs translations up without waiting for engineering.
- **Professional translation in one tool.** You want to order human translation where you edit.
- **Third-party widgets.** Much of the page comes from embeds you can't mark up.

## Moving from Weglot to General Translation

For a site built in code, run `npx gt@latest`: the [setup wizard](/docs/cli/quickstart) detects the framework and writes `gt.config.json`. Mark copy with [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t), then generate translations with [`npx gt translate`](/docs/cli/reference/commands/translate).

Keep the URLs search engines already know. If Weglot served subdirectories such as `/fr/`, `gt-next`'s middleware can serve the same prefixes, and its [`pathConfig`](/docs/react/nextjs/reference/functions/create-next-middleware#path-config) option reproduces translated slugs such as `/fr/qui-sommes-nous`. If Weglot served subdomains such as `fr.example.com`, redirect each old URL to its new one with a permanent redirect, which Google's [redirects guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/301-redirects) recommends when a page's URL changes. (See [subdirectory vs. subdomain](/kb/multilingual-url-structure) for the tradeoffs.)

Export Weglot's glossary rules (Advanced plan) and import the file into a [Context Group](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/defining-context-for-translations), which accepts CSV, TSV, JSON, and text files. Then review your highest-traffic pages in the [Dashboard](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/reviewing-translations).

## Is General Translation an alternative to Weglot?

Yes, for websites and apps whose copy lives in code. General Translation translates at the source and serves each language from your own routes, while Weglot translates the rendered page through a proxy, snippet, or plugin. For a site builder or CMS store no developer maintains, Weglot is the simpler choice.

## Other Weglot alternatives

- **Smartling:** its Global Delivery Network translates websites as a proxy, alongside AI and human translation. (See [General Translation vs. Smartling](/kb/general-translation-vs-smartling).)
- **Transifex:** Transifex Live translates a website through a JavaScript snippet. (See [General Translation vs. Transifex](/kb/general-translation-vs-transifex).)
- **Lokalise:** its LiveJS in-context editor works on websites. (See [General Translation vs. Lokalise](/kb/general-translation-vs-lokalise).)

(For CMS plugins such as WPML and site builders such as Webflow Localize, see [How do I make a website multilingual?](/kb/make-website-multilingual).)

## Key takeaways

- Weglot is a website translation layer over rendered pages; General Translation is a localization platform that translates at the source.
- Only Weglot's subdomain and subdirectory integrations give each language an indexable URL; its snippet and preview React package keep one URL per page.
- Weglot charges by translated words per language, and translation stops at the limit unless auto-upgrade is on.
- General Translation ships every language in the release, with code context, consistent terms, locale routing, translated metadata, and Dashboard review.
- Use Weglot for a CMS or site-builder site that no developer maintains.

Weglot is a website translation service. It finds the text on your rendered pages, machine-translates it, and serves each language through a CMS plugin, a JavaScript snippet, or a reverse proxy, so a site on WordPress, Shopify, or Webflow can go multilingual without anyone touching code. For an app that developers change every week, the deciding questions are different: where the translations live, whether each language gets a URL search engines can index, and how new copy gets translated before it ships.

## What Weglot does well

- **Setup without code.** Weglot's [technical overview](https://weglot.com/technical-presentation/) lists three ways in: a JavaScript snippet, a reverse proxy that serves each language on a subdomain (one CNAME record per language) or a subdirectory, and dedicated integrations for WordPress, Shopify, Wix, BigCommerce, Webflow, Squarespace, and Drupal.
- **Automatic content detection.** The proxy fetches your page from your server, detects the original text, and swaps in translations. For content that renders after the page loads, [dynamic rules](https://support.weglot.com/article/253-how-to-translate-dynamic-content) tell Weglot to re-scan the elements you choose.
- **Machine translation you can edit.** Weglot [picks DeepL, Google Translate, or Microsoft for each language pair](https://support.weglot.com/article/114-available-languages). You edit the output in its translation list or visual editor, and [glossary rules](https://support.weglot.com/article/130-translations-exceptions) make a term always or never translate.
- **Professional translation on order.** From the Starter plan up, you can [order professional translation](https://support.weglot.com/article/198-how-to-order-professional-translations) from the translation list, the URLs page, or the visual editor. Weglot says delivery takes about two business days.
- **SEO in proxy mode.** With the subdomain or subdirectory integration, each language gets its own URL, and Weglot [adds hreflang tags and translates page titles, meta descriptions, and structured data](https://support.weglot.com/article/293-how-does-weglot-manage-the-seo). [Translated URL slugs](https://support.weglot.com/article/148-can-i-translate-my-urls) need the Pro plan or higher.
- **A developer path in Weglot 2.0.** A [GitHub Action](https://developers.weglot.com/frameworks-and-ai/translate-action) translates JSON localization files and can open a pull request, and a preview React package translates JSX at build time.

## What Weglot still leaves to your engineers

Weglot is built around the rendered page. In an app your team ships from a repository, three things stay with you:

1. **Choosing a mode search engines can see.** Weglot says its JavaScript integration "does not offer SEO benefits," because translations are inserted in the browser after the page loads. Indexable languages need the subdomain or subdirectory integration, which means DNS changes and translated pages served through Weglot's proxy.
2. **Keeping translations next to the code.** Translations live in Weglot's database and are edited in its dashboard, outside your repository. [Exporting them](https://support.weglot.com/article/432-what-can-we-export-import) takes the Advanced plan or higher.
3. **Working around one URL per page in React.** [`@weglot/translate-react`](https://developers.weglot.com/frameworks-and-ai/react) detects JSX text at build time without wrapper components. It's in preview, needs a Weglot 2.0 project and React 19 (on Next.js 15 or 16.0.4 and later, or Vite), and it stores the visitor's language in a cookie. Its docs say all languages share the same URLs, so search engines index the site in its source language only.

Weglot's [pricing](https://weglot.com/pricing) is a subscription tiered by translated words and target languages. Each unique word counts once across all languages, the word limit doesn't renew monthly, and if you go over it, translation stops until you upgrade unless auto-upgrade is on. Higher tiers add seats and features such as translated URLs, import and export, and translation memory.

## Weglot vs. General Translation at a glance

| Feature | Weglot | General Translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it connects | A proxy (DNS), a JavaScript snippet, or a CMS or site-builder integration; a React package in preview | Open-source SDKs in your code (`gt-next`, `gt-react`, `gt-vue`, and others), the [`gt` CLI](/docs/cli/quickstart), and [Locadex](/docs/platform/locadex/quickstart) pull requests |
| What gets translated | Text detected on the rendered page | Content you mark with [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t) or `gt()`, plus files such as JSON, Markdown, and HTML |
| Where translations live | Weglot's database and dashboard | Files in your build or General Translation's CDN, reviewed in the Dashboard |
| Language URLs and hreflang | Subdomain or subdirectory per language with hreflang added; the snippet and React package keep one URL per page | [`createNextMiddleware`](/docs/react/nextjs/reference/functions/create-next-middleware) serves `/de/about` and localized slugs; you return hreflang and canonicals from `generateMetadata` |
| Human translation | Order from Weglot's professional translators | Your team edits in the Dashboard's Translation Editor |
| Pricing | Subscription tiers by translated words and languages | [Usage-based, with no seat charges](https://generaltranslation.com/pricing) |

## Why teams choose General Translation over Weglot

For a product developers build (a dashboard, a SaaS app, its docs), General Translation translates at the source, so the translated version is part of the build you test and ship.

- **Translations ship with the release.** Wrap copy in [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t), or let the compiler [wrap JSX automatically](/docs/cli/guides/using-auto-jsx), then run [`npx gt translate`](/docs/cli/reference/commands/translate) before `next build`. New copy is translated in the release that adds it, and [Locadex](/docs/platform/locadex/quickstart) can open the translation pull request for you.
- **Every language is indexable from your own app.** General Translation's `gt-next` middleware serves each language at its own URL, such as `/de/ueber-uns`, and [`getGT`](/docs/react/nextjs/reference/functions/get-gt) translates titles and descriptions inside `generateMetadata`. No proxy sits in front of your site. (See [language redirects and SEO](/kb/locale-routing-seo).)
- **Translations carry context from the code.** A [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t) block keeps a sentence together with its markup and variables, [`<Var>`](/docs/react/reference/components/var) keeps dynamic values out of translation entirely, and [`<Plural>`](/docs/react/reference/components/plural) and [`<Num>`](/docs/react/reference/components/num) follow each locale's rules. A Context Group's Glossary and Custom Prompts apply the same terms to your app, docs, and marketing site.
- **No runtime dependency.** General Translation's libraries are MIT-licensed and work without an account, and an app that ships translations with its build makes no network calls to General Translation at run time.

Cursor localizes its web product and docs into 14+ locales with General Translation. (See [production deployments](https://generaltranslation.com/enterprise).)

## When Weglot is the better fit

Choose Weglot when the site runs on WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, or Wix and nobody edits its code, when a marketing team needs languages live without waiting for a release, when you want to order professional translation in the same tool, or when much of the page comes from third-party widgets you can't mark up yourself. General Translation has no plugins for those site builders.

## Moving from Weglot to General Translation

For a site built in code, run `npx gt@latest`: the [setup wizard](/docs/cli/quickstart) detects the framework and writes `gt.config.json`. Mark copy with [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t), then generate translations with [`npx gt translate`](/docs/cli/reference/commands/translate).

Keep the URLs search engines already know. If Weglot served subdirectories such as `/fr/`, `gt-next`'s middleware can serve the same prefixes, and its [`pathConfig`](/docs/react/nextjs/reference/functions/create-next-middleware#path-config) option reproduces translated slugs such as `/fr/a-propos`. If Weglot served subdomains such as `fr.example.com`, redirect each old URL to its new one with a permanent redirect, which Google's [redirects guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/301-redirects) recommends when a page's URL changes. (See [subdirectory vs. subdomain](/kb/multilingual-url-structure) for the tradeoffs.)

Carry your terminology over by exporting Weglot's glossary rules (import and export need its Advanced plan) and importing the file into a [Context Group](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/defining-context-for-translations), which accepts CSV, TSV, JSON, and text files. Then review your highest-traffic pages in the [Dashboard Translation Editor](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/reviewing-translations).

## Is General Translation an alternative to Weglot?

Yes, for websites and apps whose copy lives in code. General Translation translates content at the source and serves each language from your own routes, while Weglot translates the rendered page through a proxy, snippet, or plugin. For a site builder or CMS store that no developer maintains, Weglot is the simpler choice.

## Other Weglot alternatives

Teams comparing website translation tools also look at:

- **Smartling:** its Global Delivery Network translates websites as a proxy, alongside AI and human translation from one vendor. (See [General Translation vs. Smartling](/kb/general-translation-vs-smartling).)
- **Transifex:** Transifex Live translates a website through a JavaScript snippet, and you can order human translation from its partner vendors. (See [General Translation vs. Transifex](/kb/general-translation-vs-transifex).)
- **Lokalise:** fits when designers and translators work from Figma or screenshots, and its LiveJS in-context editor works on websites. (See [General Translation vs. Lokalise](/kb/general-translation-vs-lokalise).)

(For CMS plugins such as WPML and site builders such as Webflow Localize, see [How do I make a website multilingual?](/kb/make-website-multilingual).)

## Key takeaways

- Weglot translates the rendered site through a proxy, a snippet, or a plugin, which suits marketing sites on a CMS or site builder.
- Only Weglot's subdomain and subdirectory integrations give each language its own indexable URL; its JavaScript snippet and preview React package keep one URL per page.
- Weglot charges by translated words and languages, and translation stops at the word limit unless auto-upgrade is on.
- General Translation translates at the source in your codebase, with locale routing, translated metadata, and Dashboard review, so each release ships in every language.

Use a translation API when you already own the localization pipeline. Choose General Translation when you want context, consistency checks, review, and continuous delivery managed together. AI translation has become very good: paste a page into a model and you get fluent Spanish in seconds. The hard part of localizing a product is connecting that translation to the code, reviewers, and releases that ship it.

## Localization platform vs. AI translation API

A translation API, whether a machine translation engine like DeepL or a general model like Claude, can use context, terminology, and examples you supply. Some also support translation memories. Localizing a product means connecting those capabilities to four workflows:

- **Context.** How your product works, what each string is for, your voice and terminology, and what your past translations changed and why.
- **Consistency.** The same term on page 1 and page 400, and in your app, docs, and website, with variables, plurals, and structure intact.
- **Review.** A place where your team sees each translation next to its source, edits it, leaves notes, and controls who can change what.
- **Continuous translation.** Each update translated without rerunning everything, with reviewers' edits kept and the result delivered where your team works.

With an API, your integration supplies the context and terminology, validates the results, and connects review to delivery. General Translation manages these workflows together, from content in your codebase to reviewed translations in each release.

## Which should you choose?

- **General Translation:** you want context, consistency checks, review, and delivery managed together, so your app, docs, or site ships each release in every language.
- **TMS AI:** a localization team and outside translators already run your program in Lokalise, Crowdin, or Phrase.
- **DeepL API:** you translate Word, PowerPoint, or PDF files with their formatting, or need a machine translation engine inside a system you already run.
- **Google Cloud Translation:** you're on Google Cloud and want adaptive translation tuned from your own example sentences.
- **OpenAI or Claude API:** translation is one step in a model call your product already makes, such as replying to a support ticket in the customer's language.

## General Translation: a localization platform

General Translation translates content where it's written. In React and Next.js, [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t) sends JSX as one unit with its elements, variables, and an optional [`context`](/docs/react/reference/components/t#context) note. The `gt` CLI translates JSON, YAML, Markdown, PO, iOS and Android string files, and [other formats](/docs/cli/reference/config#files), and [`tx`](/docs/node/reference/functions/tx) in `gt-node` translates content that only exists at run time.

- **Context:** [Context Groups](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/defining-context-for-translations) hold a Glossary for product names and key terms and Custom Prompts for tone, which can target one locale. They apply to every project you assign them to.
- **Consistency:** a consistency check compares new translations with existing ones. Markdown and MDX pages get a proofreading pass, then are checked against the source: headings, code blocks, links, imports, and frontmatter must match, or the translation is corrected.
- **Review:** reviewers edit translations next to their source in the [Dashboard](/docs/platform/dashboard/guides/reviewing-translations), add annotations, and browse version history. Roles control access; Enterprise plans add SSO and audit logs.
- **Continuous translation:** [`npx gt translate`](/docs/cli/reference/commands/translate) in CI translates only content whose source changed. [Locadex](/docs/platform/locadex/quickstart), an AI agent, runs on the pull requests or commits you choose and opens a pull request with the translations.

Pricing is [usage-based, with no seat charges](https://generaltranslation.com/pricing). Cursor runs General Translation in 14+ locales across web and docs. (See [production deployments](https://generaltranslation.com/enterprise).)

## TMS AI: Lokalise, Crowdin, and Phrase

Translation management systems run AI inside their editors. Lokalise's [Pro AI](https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11776521-what-is-standard-ai-mt-and-pro-ai) uses key descriptions, glossaries, style guides, and task instructions, while its Standard AI/MT tier uses Google Translate and DeepL without that context. [Crowdin AI](https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/) connects to OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic, and other providers, and its prompts can add glossary terms, translation memory suggestions, and tagged screenshots. Phrase [Language AI](https://phrase.com/platform/ai/) routes work across 30+ engines and sends low-scoring segments to review. All three work on resource files synced from your repository. (See [General Translation vs. Lokalise](/kb/general-translation-vs-lokalise), [vs. Crowdin](/kb/general-translation-vs-crowdin), and [vs. Phrase](/kb/general-translation-vs-phrase).)

## DeepL API: a machine translation engine

The [DeepL API](https://developers.deepl.com/api-reference/translate/request-translation) translates arrays of strings, each independently, and whole documents such as Word, PowerPoint, and PDF files with their formatting kept. Its `context` parameter describes the surrounding text, `formality` sets the register, a request can apply up to five [glossaries](https://developers.deepl.com/docs/customize/managing-glossaries), and [translation memories](https://developers.deepl.com/docs/customize/using-translation-memories) reuse approved translations. For an app, you still extract strings, protect placeholders, fill in extra plural forms, and write results back. (See [General Translation vs. DeepL](/kb/general-translation-vs-deepl).)

## Google Cloud Translation: neural and LLM models

[Cloud Translation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/overview) serves Google's neural machine translation (NMT) model and a [Translation LLM](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/advanced/compare-models) that Google describes as powered by Gemini. The Advanced edition adds [adaptive translation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs/advanced/adaptive-translation), which adjusts output from at least five example sentence pairs, plus glossaries, custom models, and batch translation. A request has no field for where a string appears. If you show results to users, Google requires [attribution](https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/attribution). (See [General Translation vs. Google Translate](/kb/general-translation-vs-google-translate).)

## OpenAI API and Claude API: translation inside your own prompts

LLM APIs translate fluently and follow instructions about tone, terms, and format. Both the [OpenAI API](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs) and the [Claude API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) can constrain output to a JSON schema, which helps with a batch of keys. Everything else is your code: the prompt, which glossary terms to include for each string, checks that placeholders survived, the same wording from one run to the next, and somewhere for reviewers to fix the output.

## What production localization needs around a model

Benchmarks rank engines on full documents, while an app sends short labels, sentences with variables, and plural messages. (See [Is AI translation accurate enough for a production app?](/kb/is-ai-translation-accurate))

### Short strings without context

"Save" can be German *speichern* (write a file) or *sparen* (spend less). An engine given only the word has to guess. General Translation sends each [`<T>`](/docs/react/reference/components/t) block with its surrounding elements and an optional note, and for JSON and YAML a [keyed metadata](/docs/cli/reference/keyed-metadata) file adds a note and length limit per key.

### Terms that drift

APIs can apply glossaries, translation memories, or terminology in a prompt, but your integration must keep those inputs current across requests and projects. Without that coordination, a product name can come back translated in one release and untranslated in the next. A Context Group pins terms in a Glossary and sets tone in Custom Prompts, such as formal "Sie" in German, for every assigned project. (See [How do I keep terminology consistent in AI translation?](/kb/ai-translation-glossary))

### Broken variables, plurals, and structure

Translation must keep `{name}` intact and give each language its plural forms: English has two, Arabic six. In docs, heading IDs, code blocks, and links must come back unchanged. General Translation's [`<Var>`](/docs/react/reference/components/var) keeps dynamic values out of translation, [`<Plural>`](/docs/react/reference/components/plural) gets every form the target language uses, and MDX pages are checked against their source structure.

### Edits that don't stick

A reviewer's fix should survive the next run. In the Dashboard, reviewers open **Project > Translations**, compare locales, and edit any entry, and the next Locadex run brings their edits into a pull request. (See [How do I review and edit translations?](/kb/review-translations))

## Common questions

### Is DeepL or Google Translate better for developers?

No public benchmark settles it, because WMT, the main machine translation shared task, anonymizes commercial engines. Compare them on your own strings and by what their APIs accept: DeepL has a context field, formality, and custom instructions, and Google has a Gemini-based Translation LLM and adaptive translation from example pairs. Both are APIs, so the pipeline around them is yours either way.

### Can I translate my app with ChatGPT or Claude?

Yes. Their APIs can power production localization if your pipeline supplies context and terminology, checks placeholders and plurals, and connects review to delivery. General Translation manages that workflow around the model, so your team can ship translations without maintaining those pieces itself.

### Which AI translation tool works with my existing i18n library?

All of them can. With an API, your script extracts strings from the resource files your library loads and writes translations back. TMS integrations sync the files. General Translation's CLI translates those files too: when your `package.json` declares `next-intl` or `i18next`, it [keeps that library's string syntax](/docs/cli/guides/generating-translations), so you don't have to switch libraries.

- General Translation is a localization platform for products that change every release: context from code and Context Groups, consistency and structure checks, Dashboard review, and Locadex pull requests.

To translate a Google Slides presentation, you copy the deck once per language and replace the text in every text box, table cell, and speaker note with its translation. It's hard because translated text rarely takes the same space as the original (the phrase "Save changes" runs 63% wider in German and 25% narrower in Japanese), so text overflows its box or leaves gaps that someone has to fix slide by slide. Each copy also goes out of date as soon as someone edits the source deck.

| Approach | What it does well | Where the translation ends up | What it leaves to you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Translating a copy by hand | A translator sees each slide and can shorten text to fit | A copy you make and name for each language | Every text box, table cell, and speaker note in every language, and finding what changed after each source edit |
| Google Translate | Translates a whole `.pptx` file in one upload | A downloaded `.pptx` that you convert back to Slides, which turns embedded charts and videos into images | Checking every slide for text that no longer fits, and repeating the round trip after each edit |
| A raw AI API (OpenAI API, Claude API) | Fluent translation that follows instructions | Wherever your script writes it | A script on the Google Slides API that rewrites text in shapes, table cells, and speaker notes without losing its styling, refits text that grew or shrank, and reruns after edits |
| General Translation (Google Drive integration) | Translates text in shapes, table cells, and speaker notes, keeps the source's styling, links, and lists, and runs a formatting pass, billed per slide, that fits the new text to each slide | A separate presentation per language in the source's folder, with images, videos, and charts left as they are | Checking slides where text now overflows or wraps differently, and running **Apply changes** after the source changes |

Use a hand-translated copy when one deck goes into one or two languages and a translator can fit each slide; use Google Translate when you only need to read a deck in another language, and a raw AI API only when you already script decks through the Slides API. Use General Translation's Google Drive integration when decks change and go out in several languages, so every copy has to keep its layout and follow the source.

General Translation's Google Drive integration translates presentations from the Dashboard and requires a Starter or Enterprise plan. Open **Project > Integrations > Catalog**, click **Connect** on **Google Drive**, then click **Connect account** and sign in to Google; click **Add files**, pick the presentation, check its **Source** language, choose languages under **Translate into**, and click **Translate**.

After you edit the source, **Apply changes** rebuilds each copy, reuses translations whose source text hasn't changed, and keeps your edits to a copy where the source text still matches. In General Translation, the same Context Group (a Glossary and Custom Prompts) can guide your decks, app, website, and docs, so a product name translates the same way in each. The [Google Drive Quickstart](/docs/integrations/google-drive/quickstart) has the full steps.

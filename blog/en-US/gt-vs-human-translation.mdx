Say you have an app with 50,000 words of UI copy and you want to ship it in the languages that matter. What does year one cost, and how long until the first localized release? The answer depends almost entirely on the pipeline you pick.

This post compares the two approaches teams weigh in practice: a translation management system (TMS) staffed with professional linguists, and General Translation's AI-native pipeline. Every figure is an estimated range built from published rates, linked inline. Where your situation differs, swap in your own numbers.

## The scenario

- 50,000 words of source copy, about the size of a mid-sized SaaS app.
- 17 target locales. [CSA Research forecasts](https://www.mytotalretail.com/article/content-localization-as-the-key-to-success-in-global-markets/) that 17 languages will reach 90% of internet-accessible GDP by 2027. That is a market-reach figure rather than a per-company average, but it is a fair stand-in for "the languages that matter."
- 850,000 words translated in year one (50,000 words into 17 locales).
- Engineer time costed at $700 to $900 a day, fully loaded. Estimate.

## The human pipeline: a TMS plus linguists

Your strings sync from your codebase to a TMS such as Lokalise, Crowdin, or Phrase. Professional linguists translate them in batches. Finished translations sync back and ship with your next release.

What each part costs in year one:

- **Human translation.** Published US per-word rates run [$0.15 to $0.30](https://www.smartling.com/blog/translation-rates). Agencies advertise lower: [around $0.10 a word on average](https://translated.com/translation-rates), and [$0.10 to $0.22 for common language pairs](https://thetranslationcompany.com/resources/5-facts-buying-translation/translation-pricing.htm). At 850,000 words, that is roughly $85,000 to $255,000. The same per-word rate applies again every time your copy changes.
- **The TMS itself.** Published tiers run from [about $150 a month](https://crowdin.com/pricing) through [$375 a month](https://lokalise.com/pricing) to [about $1,250 a month](https://phrase.com/pricing/), so plan on a few thousand to about fifteen thousand dollars a year.
- **Setup.** Wiring the sync, the workflows, and the review chain is typically one to two engineer-weeks.

Turnaround runs on linguist throughput. Agencies publish [about 2,000](https://www.betranslated.co.uk/translation-agency/turnaround/) to [2,500 words per day](https://www.pactranz.com/translation-times/) per linguist, so a 50,000-word catalog is four to five weeks of linguist time per language unless you parallelize across more linguists at the same total cost. Expect the first localized release a month or more after you start, and expect a new locale to mean a new vendor engagement at the same per-word rates.

## General Translation

You tag the strings in your code, AI models translate them when you deploy, and a CDN serves every locale. There is no file export and no batch handoff.

What each part costs in year one:

- **Platform.** The [Starter plan](https://generaltranslation.com/pricing) has no platform fee.
- **AI translation usage.** 850,000 words is roughly 1.1 million model tokens, which at the [published rate](https://generaltranslation.com/pricing/usage) of $1 per 1,000 input tokens comes to about $1,000 to $1,500, depending on how your content tokenizes.
- **Integration.** Tagging strings and adding config is half a day to a day of engineer time for a typical Next.js or React app.

New content translates automatically on the next deploy, and adding a locale is one line of config.

## Side by side

|  | TMS plus linguists | General Translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year-one cost | $90,000 to $280,000 | $1,000 to $2,500 |
| First localized release | a month or more | the same day |
| 50,000 new words | weeks of linguist time per language | on the next deploy |
| Adding a locale | a new vendor engagement | one config line |

The year-one totals sum each pipeline's parts above: translation, platform, and engineering for the TMS path; usage and integration for General Translation.

## Assumptions

- One English word is counted as 1.33 model tokens, OpenAI's published rule of thumb. Estimate.
- The ranges come from the vendor pages linked above, fetched in August 2026. Vendors that publish no price for a tier are left out.
- Human translation quality and AI translation quality are not compared here; this post is about cost and turnaround.

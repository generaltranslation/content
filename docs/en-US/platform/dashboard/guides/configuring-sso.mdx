Configure single sign-on (SSO) at Organization or Enterprise scope, then verify the email domain before requiring members to use it.

## Before you start [#before-start]

- Your plan must include SSO. Otherwise, the SSO page shows the required upgrade.
- You need permission to manage the Organization or Enterprise. This is normally an **Admin**.
- Gather your identity provider's SAML metadata or OpenID Connect (OIDC) credentials.

## Configuring a provider [#provider]

1. Open **Organization > Settings > SSO** or **Enterprise > Settings > SSO**.
2. Choose **Configure SSO**.
3. Select **SAML** or **OIDC**.
4. Enter the provider settings described below.
5. Add any attribute mappings your identity provider requires.
6. Save the configuration.

### a) SAML

The dialog shows values to copy into your identity provider:

- **Single sign-on URL (ACS URL)**
- **Audience URI (SP Entity ID)**
- **SP Metadata URL**

Enter the email **Domain**, then provide identity provider metadata in one of these forms:

- **Metadata URL** — the Dashboard fetches the XML when you save.
- **Metadata XML** — paste or upload the XML.
- **Manual** — enter the identity provider sign-on URL and certificate.

Turn on signed authentication requests only when your identity provider requires them.

### b) OpenID Connect

Copy the displayed **Callback URL (Redirect URI)** into your identity provider. Enter:

- **Issuer**
- **Domain**
- **Client ID**
- **Client secret**
- **Discovery endpoint** when your provider does not use the issuer's standard discovery location

## Verifying the domain [#verify-domain]

The provider remains unverified until the Dashboard finds the DNS TXT record.

1. Copy the exact **Record name / host** shown in the Dashboard. Add the TXT record at that host; do not use `@` or the root domain.
2. Copy the raw **TXT record value** into the record value field.
3. Wait for DNS propagation.
4. Choose **Verify Domain**.

Verification tokens expire after one week. If a token expires, choose **Request New Token**, replace the TXT value, and verify again.

## Requiring SSO [#enforcement]

After the domain is verified, turn on **Require SSO**. Members with an email address on that domain can then sign in only through the configured identity provider; other sign-in methods are blocked.

Turn the setting off before changing identity provider configuration when members need another sign-in path during maintenance.

## Managing SAML signing keys [#signing-keys]

When signed SAML authentication requests are enabled:

1. Choose **View Certificate** under **SP Signing Certificate**.
2. Upload that certificate to your identity provider.

**Regenerate key pair** creates a new signing key pair and permanently replaces the old pair. After regeneration, upload the new certificate to your identity provider before relying on signed requests.

## Updating or removing SSO [#update-remove]

Choose **Edit SSO Config** to update the existing provider without changing its protocol. Choose **Remove SSO** to delete the provider configuration.
